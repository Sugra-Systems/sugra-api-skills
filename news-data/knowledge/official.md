# Official records

## Sanctions and watchlists
Anchor: official.sanctions_match
Companions: official.sanctions_sources, official.watchlist_match, news.adverse_media
Why together: A screen is only as good as the lists loaded and their freshness; adverse media shows allegations no list carries.
Methods (the answer names the one it uses):
- A name match is a candidate, never an identification: confirm with a second identifier (date of birth, nationality, registration number, address) and state the fields that matched.
- Name each list screened and its last update. A clear result reads "no match on the lists screened as of <date>", never "not sanctioned"; a result downgraded for a stale list stays "review".
- Search the native script, transliterations and known aliases.
- A designation in the news is confirmed by the list entry and its listing date; announced is not in force.
- Under US Treasury rules an entity owned 50 percent or more by listed parties is blocked without being listed: a list screen does not show it.
- Adverse media is coverage: allegation, charge and conviction are three different things; name which the source reports.
Do not:
- Call a screening result legal clearance.

## Legislation and official acts
Anchor: official.bill_status
Companions: official.enacted_laws, official.hearings, official.legislative_record, official.regulatory_notices
Why together: News says a bill "passed"; the record says which stage, which chamber and on what date.
Methods (the answer names the one it uses):
- Stages: introduced, reported by committee, passed one chamber, passed both, signed or enacted. Quote the latest action and its date from the record.
- A US bill number restarts with each Congress: name the Congress with the number.
- Enactment is not effect: give the effective date when the text sets one. A proposed rule is not a final rule.
- The official summary lags the text; a later text version replaces an earlier one.
Do not:
- Report an announcement or a vote in one chamber as a law.
