# International

## Comparing countries
Anchor: international.gdp_growth
Companions: international.country_panel, international.harmonised_inflation, international.harmonised_unemployment, rates.policy_rate, prices.cpi_core, international.official_forecasts, expectations.professional_survey
Why together: Definitions and release dates differ: harmonised series make levels comparable, national releases are newer.
Methods (the answer names the one it uses):
- The same indicators and one base date for every country; a country without the figure shows "no data", not a substitute.
- The latest date per country; forecasts apart from actuals.
- A panel of several countries is quick but may be undated and lag a monthly figure: use it for a first comparison, then date each figure from its own country series when the question asks for a date or the freshest value.
- Harmonised series (IMF, OECD, World Bank, ILO definitions) for levels, national releases for the latest value; say which.
- Levels in one currency name the conversion: market exchange rates or purchasing power parity.
- Forecasts of the IMF, OECD and central banks side by side, each with its edition date; none is a consensus.
- Euro area: ECB order (prices file); flash first, full release mid-month; projections against the previous round.
- UK: ONS order (labour file); real pay names its deflator.
- Japan: Tankan diffusion indexes are balances in pp (good minus bad), actual beside the firms' forecast.
- Canada: headline against the two preferred core measures (trim, median; common is heavily revised); an employment change against its standard error: beyond one it is real about two times in three, beyond two by the 95 percent rule; two months in one direction weigh more.
- Australia: ABS order (prices file); say whether each monthly figure is seasonally adjusted.
- Brazil: surprise = actual minus the central bank survey median; drift of expectations over 1 and 4 weeks; ex ante real rate = policy rate minus expected inflation; survey horizons are calendar years, so match them to the 12-month series.
- Other countries (Mexico, India and the rest): the general rules apply.
Traps:
- Unemployment and price index definitions differ across countries.
- US quarterly growth annualised against unannualised growth elsewhere.
Do not:
- Present an international database series as national statistics.
- Rank countries on figures for different periods without saying so.
