# Industry

## Production assets in a hazard zone
Anchor: industry.offshore_shut_in
Companions: industry.refineries, industry.refinery_status, power.plants, hazards.tropical_cyclones, prices.crude_oil, prices.gasoline, commodities.natgas_price, commodities.futures_curve, commodities.crude_stocks, commodities.product_stocks, commodities.energy_outlook, growth.industrial_production
Why together: Where a storm passes matters more than its strength: the share of output inside the storm-force wind field is the number for oil and gas.
Methods (the answer names the one it uses):
- Exposure: assets on the path, distance from the centre against the hurricane-force wind radius.
- Shut-in share of the area's output, against the seasonal loss the official outlook already assumes, not against zero.
- US Gulf analogues (BSEE shut-in statistics): a category 1 over the main producing area took 18 percent of a month's oil, a category 4 on its edge 6 percent (2017); a direct hit shut 96 percent of oil and 94 of gas (2021). The coast holds about 55 percent of US refining (EIA).
- Signs: shut-in lifts crude; refinery outages lift gasoline and diesel and cut crude demand; closed export terminals and power cuts cut domestic gas demand, so gas can fall while oil rises.
Traps:
- Platforms evacuated are a count, not an output share.
- Refining can stop before landfall; onshore damage outlasts platform shut-ins.
- A list of plants by emissions is exposure, not operating status.
