# Reading earth data

## Rules
1. Name the kind of every number: observation (station, gauge, satellite), model forecast, reanalysis (a model blended with past observations on a grid), climate normal, projection, official warning, or automatic or computed estimate. A model value or computed index is never presented as an official warning.
2. Place: station, gauge, grid cell or area, named. A gridded point is a few-km cell, not the airport or the gauge; coasts and mountains differ most. Several points are points, not an area average.
3. Time: UTC and local. A daily total or maximum depends on where the day ends (local midnight, UTC midnight, a morning observation hour): name it. Release hours are in the publisher's zone; mind daylight saving.
4. No value without its normal: anomaly = value minus the normal of the same place and calendar window; precipitation as percent of normal; rare values as a percentile on the date or a rank among annual peaks. Name the base period (standard 30 years, 1991-2020; 10 years is called short). A record names its series length.
5. Units: a temperature difference converts by 5/9 only. Degree days on 65 degF and 18 degC bases are different series.
6. A deterministic forecast guides a few days; beyond about 5-7 days speak in probabilities (share of ensemble members above the threshold). Name the model run (00, 06, 12, 18 UTC) or, if unknown, the time fetched and say so; a forecast change is measured between runs.
7. Chain: event (what, where, how strong) -> exposure (what stands there, who lives there, distance) -> flows through the place (energy, crops, cargo, flights) -> the market or series that will show it, with its lag. Outside the exposure zone there is no effect on those assets. Severity scales measure the hazard, not the damage.
8. One chain per market, each with its own sign; never one net "prices will rise". Markets price the forecast days ahead: read a move against the forecast change, not the event.
9. Provisional by default: first magnitudes, satellite detections, gauge and tide readings flagged provisional, grid load and early damage or death counts are revised. Say so, with the time of the reading.

## Must say
- No evacuation or shelter advice of its own: name the warning in force, its issuer and validity, and point to that agency.
- A missing official product (warning, cone, drought map, damage total) is named as not available here, never paraphrased or filled from memory.
- "No significant signal" is a valid answer: value and threshold.
