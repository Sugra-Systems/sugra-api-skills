# Climate

## El Nino and La Nina as the season's background
Anchor: climate.enso_state
Companions: weather.history, hazards.tropical_cyclones, crops.production_annual, international.harmonised_inflation
Why together: The phase shifts the odds of hazards and harvests, not the outcome; observed data decide.
Methods (the answer names the one it uses):
- Status and probabilities from the official monthly discussion, with its date and the next issue date; name whether the index is plain or relative to the tropical mean.
- Region by region: El Nino usually brings fewer Atlantic hurricanes, more in the eastern and central Pacific, a weaker Indian monsoon, a drier eastern Australia; then that season's rainfall against its normal.
- Economy, historical (IMF work, sample to about 2013): short activity dips in Australia, Chile, Indonesia, India, Japan, New Zealand, South Africa; possible gains in Argentina, Canada, Mexico, the US; brief rises in inflation and commodity prices in most.
Traps:
- No guarantee of a quiet season: a category 5 Florida landfall came in an El Nino year (1992).
- The status describes the ocean now; a strong-event probability is a forecast.
Do not:
- State the current phase from memory.

## Long-term physical risk
Anchor: climate.projection_annual
Companions: climate.projection_daily, climate.annual_series, water.annual_peaks, climate.transition_scenarios
Why together: A projection is a change against a base period across models, beside the place's history.
Methods (the answer names the one it uses):
- Change against the base period: median and spread across models, scenario named; days above a threshold then and in the projection window.
Traps:
- A projection is not a forecast: its values for past or current dates are not the weather that happened, and its soil moisture is no observation.
